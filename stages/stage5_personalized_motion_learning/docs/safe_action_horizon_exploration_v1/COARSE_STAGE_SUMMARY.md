The frozen 120-pattern coarse grid completed across four V3 checkpoints (Rep2, 6, 16, 26), six direction families, three amplitudes and H1–H4. Four unmodified checkpoint replays exactly reproduced the formal baseline J_F_task and passed physical/scientific gates. Of 120 exploration attempts, 75 were valid, 40 were rejected as infeasible, five were physically invalid, and none had an infrastructure exception. Two bounded infrastructure repairs occurred before this formal coarse grid: preserving the phase-local decision index across worker snapshots and recording safety-rejected incomplete episodes as infeasible. Both have regression tests; the early pilots and failed pilot records remain in ignored raw storage.

The best coarse valid Benefit was 85.026496 N s (16.9990%) at the Rep5 checkpoint, faster/large/H4. Its full task J_F fell from 500.185485 to 415.158989 N s, with physical and scientific PASS. The first 1.5 s instead worsened by 5.301293 N s. The task finished 0.945 s sooner; mean task force rose by about 0.047 N, while peak force rose and deployable clearance fell modestly. This is evidence for duration-mediated interaction integral headroom, not lower instantaneous force.

Twelve valid patterns improved full-task J_F; their median positive Benefit was 38.185560 N s. All twelve were faster-progress actions outside the first-decision baseline candidate convex hull. The short-window versus full-task Benefit Spearman correlation across valid coarse samples was -0.913997 and within-checkpoint pairwise ranking agreement was 0.109610. These exploratory paired comparisons support a long-horizon credit problem, without a statistical significance claim.

All 45 rejected/invalid samples concerned existing clearance/geometry limits. Knee-dominant yielded no valid sample in this grid. All frozen production source hashes still match V3.
