# WSL learning scientific stability diagnosis

Category: development validation. Existing Mac and WSL raw evidence was used; no cross-host campaign was rerun.

## Low-ROM initial IK

At the ordinary low-ROM registered start, all accepted candidates have pose residual below `1e-8`. The two top physical joint branches have minimum attachment-Jacobian singular value `0.29536411919229405` on this WSL host. The old exact `max` chose seed 21, joint state approximately `[1.027715, 1.079877, -2.237415, 2.591622, -1.721008, 1.542185]`. Another candidate at seed 23 has the same printed primary score and joint state approximately `[-2.113878, -1.079877, 2.237415, -0.549971, -1.721008, 1.542185]`. Their Euclidean distances from the existing CR12 nominal home posture are `4.391476166666459` and `4.0352580358501635` rad. The old choice thus depended on numerical SVD rounding and candidate order. The historical loaded initial q from Mac and WSL also differs by this branch symmetry, with minimum singular values separated by about `3.2e-15`; the individual Mac candidate scores were not saved.

The new rule retains the maximum minimum singular value as the primary objective. Candidates within `64 * float64 epsilon * max(1, abs(best_score))` of the best score are treated as unresolved by numerical precision of the 6×6 Jacobian SVD. Among these, select minimum Euclidean distance to the existing nominal home posture, using the same 64-epsilon numerical resolution for distance ties, then the lexicographic joint vector. The score window is about `1.42e-14` here, while the next distinct score is about `0.0105` lower. Clear primary winners retain their old selection. On this WSL host the new rule chooses the seed-23 branch. This rule was chosen from IK numerical resolution and existing nominal posture semantics, not to match the Mac branch.

Focused tests cover exact primary ties, near ties with reversed candidate input order, a clear primary winner, and an exact secondary tie. CR12 model regression tests also pass. The rule applies only to initial `previous_q_rad is None`; continuous IK remains unchanged.

## High-ROM hip and 120/120 synchronous

The two cases share the same initial path. In both, native step 1 has maximum absolute q/dq/torque Mac–WSL differences approximately `1.48e-14` rad, `5.73e-14` rad/s and `2.59e-12` Nm. At step 20 (`0.005 s`), these are `1.51e-14`, `5.65e-13`, and `2.59e-12`. The scheduled control update at step 21 (`0.00525 s`) raises them to `3.42e-14`, `1.35e-10`, and `1.45e-7`; the first old-tolerance breach is torque component 0 at `5.98e-8` Nm. By `0.05 s`, q/dq/torque maximum absolute differences are `2.09e-10`, `1.90e-8`, and `1.82e-6`.

The torque is held constant over the first 20 native steps and recomputed at the ordinary 5 ms control boundary. Contact pairs remain empty over the examined 0–0.05 s interval. The step-21 command `[20.195, 146.306, 33.019, -11.346, -10.570, 19.056]` Nm is within the CR12 torque limits `[436, 436, 194, 102, 66, 66]`; no clipping threshold explains the jump. The saved full-run comparison reports identical native contact sequences, waypoint labels, phase transition times, request/version vectors, and scorer-v2 condition booleans for both cases. The difference is classified **A: smooth floating-point drift amplified by a scheduled continuous control recomputation**. No distinct event, branch flip, or solver-dependent discrete choice was identified. No high-ROM production change is justified. The historical cross-host tolerance failure remains recorded; it is not a WSL same-host acceptance threshold.
